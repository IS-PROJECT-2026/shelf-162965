Frankenstein

Mary Shelley, 1818

Gothic

Victor Frankenstein, a young natural philosopher consumed by the question of what animates living matter, assembles a creature from salvaged human remains and brings it to life. Repulsed by what he has made, he abandons it. The creature, abandoned and articulate, learns language and grief in isolation, and returns to demand that its maker answer for the act of creation.

Shelley began the novel at nineteen, during a wet summer at Lake Geneva. It is usually read as the first work of science fiction, though it is as much a book about responsibility as about science.
